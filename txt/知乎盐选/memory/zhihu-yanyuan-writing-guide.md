# Zhihu Yanyuan Writing Guide

Source: official guidance copied by the user. Apply this as the default editorial checklist for Zhihu Yanyuan writing in this workspace.

## Platform Logic

- Zhihu is a Q&A platform. A story answer must fit the question instead of using an unrelated clickbait opening.
- Click-through rate depends heavily on the first two visible lines.
- The lead is critical: aim for about 40 characters, then see whether the core hook can be sharpened to about 20 characters.
- The lead and the body must deliver the same promise.

## Lead Checklist

A strong lead should:

- stay relevant to the question;
- open directly with the most distinctive setting or conflict;
- reveal a reversal or unresolved danger that makes readers ask why and what happens next;
- trigger an emotion such as curiosity, tension, anticipation, sympathy, or personal identification;
- place the reader inside the protagonist's point of view.

## Story Requirements

- The concept should be explainable in one sentence.
- Open quickly and establish the story's tone, core desire, and unusual element without delay.
- Build the middle around a core mystery, character tension, and a fresh treatment of familiar elements.
- Prefer a new perspective or new solution for old story ingredients: "the same thing, but different."
- Close the loop: show how the protagonist faces and resolves the central problem, then give the reader emotional release and a resonant aftertaste.

## Format And Style

- Typical length: 10,000-30,000 Chinese characters.
- Keep the main plot clear and continuous; keep subplots easy to follow.
- Use reversals and suspense throughout.
- Keep pacing brisk and avoid padding.
- Give characters a clear growth path and emotional credibility.
- Write in accessible, fluent, conversational Chinese with clean paragraphing.

## Topic, Theme, And Selling Point

- Choose topics where interest, accumulated understanding, and platform demand overlap.
- Draw themes from life while elevating them beyond the literal event.
- Do not rely on vulgarity, cheap shock, or divisive posturing.
- Combine content novelty with craft: a distinctive premise, memorable characters, repeated reversals, and a hook in each section.

## Character Design

- Keep relationships clean and forceful.
- Introduce no more than three significant characters at the opening.
- Make characters specific, believable, and distinctive.
- Use extremity, contrast, or later reversal when appropriate.

## Structural Checkpoints

- First 200 characters: identify the main plot and strongest attraction.
- First 1,000 characters: make the protagonist's desire and motivation clear.
- First 3,000 characters: plant the central suspense.
- By the first third or halfway point: deliver a major reversal or smaller climax.
- During development: accelerate conflict and aim for at least one meaningful attraction point per 1,000 characters.
- At the climax: deliver the largest reversal and connect development to resolution.
- At the ending: clearly account for major character outcomes, close the plot loop, and deepen the theme without becoming preachy.

## Suspense Pattern

- Opening: raise the question.
- Development: intensify the question.
- Climax: answer the question.
- The answer should feel surprising but logically earned.

## Working Review Checklist

Before considering a draft ready:

1. Can the premise be stated in one sentence?
2. Do the first two lines earn the click while staying faithful to the question?
3. Is the lead concise, emotionally charged, and written from the protagonist's perspective?
4. Do the first 200, 1,000, and 3,000 characters meet their structural jobs?
5. Is there a fresh treatment of a familiar element?
6. Does each major section contain a hook, reveal, conflict escalation, or reversal?
7. Does the ending resolve the central danger and leave a meaningful aftertaste?
